Approach

At times, life can be complicated, confusing and overwhelming. Whether you're navigating relationship challenges, addictive behaviours, bereavement, or simply feeling stuck, having a discussion with a professional can provide practical insights.

My approach combines philosophical-style counselling with practical coaching strategies. Rather than relying on one therapeutic model, we'll draw on ancient wisdom to deepen self knowledge, build resilience and confidence.

About

Hi, I'm Zoe. Alongside a Master of Counselling, I hold a Bachelor of Philosophy. Both have shaped how I understand people. While psychology helps explain behaviour, philosophy asks different questions: What gives life meaning? How do we respond to suffering? What kind of person do we want to become? I bring over fifteen years' experience working alongside adolescents and adults navigating trauma, addiction, the criminal justice system and bereavement.

Through this complex work, I've seen that change rarely comes from endlessly talking. Rather than offering answers, I aim to meet people where they are, understand their unique story, and help them explore their own way forward.

I aim to provide an open space where our conversations are genuine and unfiltered. Our minds have ways of spiralling, and sometimes a single sentence can untangle a complex problem. Whatever has brought you here, there is always a way forward.

Values

I don't see people as problems to be solved. I believe people make sense once their story makes sense. Effective counselling should be practical, honest, collaborative, short-term, insightful and individualised — focused on meaningful change rather than endless reflection.

I'm interested in our ability to adapt and find meaning through challenges. Outside the therapy room, when I'm not working, you'll usually find me gardening, hiking or freediving.

Freediving has become more than a hobby. It has taught me that slowing the breath changes the mind, that calm can be practised, and that depth is reached gradually — not by force, but through patience and presence. Those lessons naturally find their way into my work with clients.

Frequently Asked Questions
01What happens in the first session?

We'll spend time getting to know each other — what's brought you here, what you're hoping to get out of it, and how I work. No pressure to have it all figured out.

02Is counselling confidential?

Yes. What you share stays private, with the standard legal exceptions around risk of serious harm to yourself or others — I'll explain these clearly at the outset.

03What's the difference between counselling and coaching?

Counselling tends to focus on understanding and working through difficult experiences, thoughts and emotions. Coaching is focused more on goal setting, motivation, practical steps. Most people benefit from a blend of both.

04How long are sessions?

Standard sessions run for 50 minutes, billed at the hourly rate. Longer or shorter sessions can be arranged.

05Do you offer online appointments?

Yes — all sessions are online, so you can access support from anywhere. Face to face appointments are available by request.

06Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to book. As counselling isn't currently eligible for Medicare rebate, a GP Mental Health Treatment Plan isn't required either.

07How much does it cost?

Sessions are AU$90 per hour — sliding scale available.

08Can I claim rebates?

Not through Medicare at present — counsellors aren't yet included under the Better Access scheme, though the ACA is actively advocating for this to change. Some private health extras policies do cover counselling; it's worth checking your specific fund.

09What if I'm not sure counselling is right for me?

That's what the free 15-minute consultation is for — a friendly chat to see if I'm the right fit for you.
