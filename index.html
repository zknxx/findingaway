Hi, I'm Zoe

I'm interested in your unique experience of life and what brings meaning. I don't see our conversations as only managing symptoms or fixing what's broken. It's rather a space to ask deeper questions: what do you value? Does your life feel chosen and decided, or inherited? Do you feel buried beneath the expectations of others?

I've taken an unconventional path through life that has shaped my interest in people, behaviour and the questions that don't always have straightforward answers. In my practice, I combine my studies of philosophy, psychology, and a Master's of Counselling. With a professional background in youth crime and social work, I value dynamic, honest talk over excessive assessments. I aim to meet people where they are, understanding their story as they find their own way forward.

Approach

Sometimes the parts of ourselves we overlook, avoid or reject have more influence over our lives than we realise.

My approach combines counselling with philosophy and practical strategies. I'm interested in the relationship between how we think, what we experience, how we respond and the choices we make.

That includes the things we don't always recognise as stress. The constant worrying, the unsettled stomach, the exhaustion, the feeling that something is wrong without quite knowing what. Not everything is immediately obvious, and not everything needs to be labelled.

I don't see people as problems to be solved. But I do believe we can become very good at avoiding the things that might actually move us forward. The aim is not endless analysis. It's to understand ourselves and our circumstances well enough to do something differently.

On freediving

Freediving has influenced how I think about discomfort, uncertainty and control. Underwater, you encounter sensations you can't simply switch off. You learn to distinguish sensation from interpretation, and become aware of how your response changes the experience. There's a difference between experiencing discomfort and becoming consumed by it.

What do we notice? What do we ignore? What do we automatically react to? And what happens when we start paying attention? These ideas quietly inform the way I approach conversations.

Questions

What happens in the first session?

We'll spend time getting to know each other, what's brought you here, what you're hoping to get out of it, and how I work. No pressure to have it all figured out.

Is counselling confidential?

Yes. What you share stays private, with the standard legal exceptions around risk of serious harm to yourself or others. I'll explain these clearly at the outset.

How long are sessions?

Standard sessions run for 50 minutes, billed at the hourly rate. Longer or shorter sessions can be arranged.

Do you offer online appointments?

Yes, all sessions are online, so you can access support from anywhere. Face to face appointments are available by request.

Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to book. As counselling isn't currently eligible for Medicare rebate, a GP Mental Health Treatment Plan isn't required either.

Can I claim rebates?

Not through Medicare at present. Counsellors aren't yet included under the Better Access scheme, though the ACA is actively advocating for this to change. Some private health extras policies do cover counselling; it's worth checking your specific fund.

What if I'm not sure counselling is right for me?

That's what the free 15-minute consultation is for: a friendly chat to see if I'm the right fit for you.
